# Vision

Educational software keeps making the same trade: the richer the question bank, the
bigger the company between a kid and their curiosity — accounts, subscriptions,
progress locked in someone else's cloud. Quiz Me is the education pillar of the
starter shelf, and its answer is a division of labour: the **agent** is the
question-writer, able to set a five-question quiz on literally any topic a kid can
type — dinosaurs, volcanoes, whatever this week's obsession is — and the **app** is
the referee, validating every reply hard and running the quiz itself.

That split is the point. The brain is creative and untrusted; the referee is boring
and absolute. A reply that is not exactly five well-formed questions is rejected
wholesale, and the built-in practice bank steps in with an honest note — so a
keyless first visit still gets a complete quiz, and the demo never depends on a
model being present.

The emotional core is the history card: every score lands in a real SQLite table in
a file the family owns, and renders as a little bar chart of past rounds. Not
gamification — just a kid watching themselves climb, on a record nobody else holds.
